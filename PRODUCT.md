# Culinaria

## Register

product

## Users

Home cooks use Culinaria while deciding what to make, managing ingredients, and following recipes in the kitchen. They range from beginners to confident cooks and may need to account for allergies, health conditions, cultural food practices, limited time, limited equipment, or texture requirements. The interface should remain easy to operate on a phone or tablet when attention and dexterity are divided.

## Product Purpose

Culinaria turns available ingredients and practical constraints into complete recipes, then helps users discover dishes, maintain a pantry, save favorites, and assemble a weekly meal plan. Success means users can move from “what can I make?” to a trustworthy, cookable plan with minimal friction.

## Brand Personality

Warm, capable, and inclusive. The product should feel like a knowledgeable cooking companion: appetizing without being precious, confident without sounding clinical, and helpful without over-explaining.

## Anti-references

- Clinical calorie trackers that reduce food to metrics.
- Novelty AI interfaces that foreground the technology instead of the cooking task.
- Luxury editorial food sites that trade legibility and speed for ornament.
- Dense enterprise dashboards or generic card-heavy SaaS layouts.

## Design Principles

1. Start with what the cook already has and the constraints they actually face.
2. Make dietary and accessibility needs first-class inputs, never afterthoughts.
3. Keep AI behind the task: show clear actions, useful progress, and recoverable errors.
4. Design for divided attention with readable hierarchy, generous targets, and persistent state.
5. Preserve the pleasure of cooking through warm language and appetizing presentation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard and screen-reader operation, visible focus, reduced motion, sufficient color contrast, and 44px touch targets on compact screens. Never rely on color alone for pantry status, selections, progress, or errors. Dietary output is assistive guidance, not medical advice, and the interface should make that distinction clear when relevant.
